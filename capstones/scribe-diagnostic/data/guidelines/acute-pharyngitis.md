Most sore throats are viral and resolve without antibiotics. Use the Centor criteria to decide on testing: fever above 38°C, tonsillar exudate, tender anterior cervical lymphadenopathy, and absence of cough score one point each.

With a Centor score of 0 or 1, do not test or treat with antibiotics. With a score of 2 or 3, perform a rapid streptococcal antigen test and treat only if positive. With a score of 4, test or treat empirically.

First-line treatment for confirmed streptococcal pharyngitis is penicillin V for ten days. In patients with a documented penicillin allergy, use a macrolide or a first-generation cephalosporin if the allergy was not anaphylaxis; the chart's allergy list decides, not recall in the room.

Advise analgesia, fluids and rest for every patient, and review if symptoms persist beyond seven days or worsen.
